## R1

> For optimal performance, the relay requires accurate values of positive- and zero-sequence impedances for the protected transmission line. The relay uses positive-sequence impedance settings to establish phase distance element reaches and calculate fault locations. Similarly, the relay uses zero-sequence impedances with positive-sequence impedances to establish ground distance element reaches and to calculate line-ground fault locations.
>
> — p. 5-5 (PDF page 129; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

## CTR

## PSVC

> Enable the voltage checking function by selecting a PSVC setting of S, P, or E. Voltage checking is disabled when you select PSVC = N. ## 25T

> If the 25T time is relatively short, the breaker close time may become a factor. For example, if 25T is set for 60 cycles and the breaker close time is 10 cycles, it actually takes 70 cycles to parallel the line. If this time is critical, the breaker close time should be taken into account when the 25T time is set so that, in this case, a 25T setting of 50 cycles might be more appropriate.
>
> — p. 5-10 (PDF page 134; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

> To set the relay, you must know the maximum angle (∅) between VPOL and VS that is allowed for the breaker to close. The slip frequency should be estimated to set the 25T time delay and the breaker close time should also be known.

## Z1%

> The Zone 1 reach is typically set short of the forward remote terminal. Thus, the Zone 1 elements provide instantaneous protection for phase-phase, three-phase, and phase-ground faults in the first 75-80% of the transmission line. The remainder of the line is protected by the overreaching Zone 2 elements. While the percent error of the impedance element reach is less than 5%, errors in the CT and PT ratios, modeled transmission line data, and fault study data do not permit Zone 1 element settings of 100%.
>
> — p. 5-13 (PDF page 137; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

> Z1 Reach = 0.80 x 81.16 Ω primary @ 80.1° = 64.93 Ω primary @ 80.1°  
> The reach settings for the phase distance elements are a percentage of the positive-sequence line impedance settings along the line angle. When the MTA setting differs from the positive-sequence line angle, the relay calculates the mho circle diameter with the following equation:  
> Diameter = Set Reach / cos(Angle of Z1 - MTA)
>
> — p. 5-14 (PDF page 138; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

> For the relay at Breaker 3:  
> Z1% = 80.0
>
> — p. 5-14 (PDF page 138; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

## Z2%

> The Zone 2 phase and ground distance elements provide coverage for end-of-section faults. The time delay allows coordination between the Zone 2 element of this relay and the Zone 1 element of the next relay in the forward direction.
>
> — p. 5-14 (PDF page 138; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

> Zone 2 elements should never extend past the Zone 1 reach of the next line terminal. This prevents race conditions between Zone 2 time delayed elements of the two line terminals. Typical settings for the Zone 2 distance elements are 120 - 130% of the protected line.
>
> — p. 5-15 (PDF page 139; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

> Zone 2 element settings with a reach of 120% of protected line impedance account for the effects of infeed. This point must be verified using a fault study to calculate the apparent ohms at the local terminal for a fault at the remote end of the transmission line. … Assuming Breaker 5 Zone 1 reach is set for 80% of the line impedance of Line 3, verify that a Zone 2 reach of 120% for Breaker 3 does not overreach the Zone 1 elements at Breaker 5.  
> Line 2 Impedance + [0.8 x Line 3 Impedance] = 81.6 Ω + [0.8 x 40.58 Ω]  
> = 113.62 Ω primary  
> When Zone 2 at Breaker 3 has a set reach of 120%, the effective reach is:  
> Zone 2 @ Bkr. 3 = 1.20 x 81.16 Ω = 97.39 Ω along the line angle  
> Since 97.39 Ω < 113.62 Ω, the Zone 2 setting of 120% at Breaker 3 does not overreach the Zone 1 protection of Line 3. Any effect of infeed tends to increase the apparent ohms seen at Breaker 3 for faults on Line 3.
>
> — p. 5-15 (PDF page 139; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)

> The primary limit check allows Zone 2 percent reach settings of 0 - 3200% with the requirement that the Zone 2 reach be equal to or greater than the Zone 1 reach. The secondary check allows secondary reach settings of 0.125 - 64 ohms secondary along the MTA. … The Z2% setting of 120 for this example lies within the secondary setting limits of the relay. … Zone 2 must be less than Zone 3. The Z2PT and Z2GT bits in the Relay Word depend on the Z2% setting.
>
> — p. 5-16 (PDF page 140; transcribed from the page image, 2026-09-23)
